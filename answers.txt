    Pros:
        1. Increased adaptability during product development due to multiple iterations of revising the planned design based on prior feedback/review.
        2. Better potential for upholding the expected functionality of the stakeholder and spec, as functionality can be tweaked mid-development.

    Cons:
        1. Not always compatible with certain projects that might require predictable, set development timelines or resource limitations.
        2. Can potentially occur in drifting from the original project expectations due to a lesser emphasis on the initial planning/design of the product.

Question b)
    Scrum Master:
        - Responsible for leading the agile development teams and thus the overall direction of the development of the product itself.
        - Manages team meetings and design reviews to ensure the product is still set to fulfill its active milestone.
    
    Product Owner:
        - Reviews the deliverable of each major milestone in the product development lifecycle.
        - Directly interacts with product stakeholders/customers to obtain the project requirements, and communicates those to the Scrum Master.
        - Responsible for ensuring the final deliverable of the product adheres to expectations/requirements.

Question c)
    3 Types of meetings during a sprint cycle:
        1. Sprint Planning
            - To discuss at the beginning of a sprint what must be worked on during the sprint.
            - Creates the backlog to structure exactly how the work must be done.

        2. Daily Scrum
            - Discuss/review current progress towards the goal of the sprint.
            - Edit the sprint backlog to account for the upcoming day, based off the insights gained from the review.

        3. Sprint Review
            - Review the state of the sprint goal at the end of the sprint, i.e. whether it met expectations or not.
            - Get stakeholder feedback on the current progress of the product, so that it can be applied to the upcoming sprint.

Question d)
    JIRA issue types:
        - Epic: The broadest issue type that contains Story, Bug, and Task issues. Epic issues represent significant, high-level product features/requirements.
        
        - Story: An issue in-between the high-level significance of an Epic and low-level Tasks. Represents a small/medium-sized portion of work that accounts for the portion of the development of an Epic (or non-Epic, broader feature).

        - Task: An issue type representing a low-level, typically small piece of work that contributes to an Epic or Story.

        - Bug: A feature or element of the product that is currently malfunctioning or misaligned with the project requirements and needs fixing.
